# The Long Century · Mobile overhaul plan

**Design brief for a personal, offline, portrait grand-strategy game, 1836–1936.** Start from Long_Century_Patched_Source.zip. Build one country deeply enough to enjoy for fifteen years, then carry the same rules across the world. This document is a design and implementation backlog. Numerical thresholds below are proposed acceptance targets.

## Design decisions from diaries #1–195

The reference sequence begins with population in #1 and ends with the *State and Revolution* 1.15 changelog in #195. The numbered register at the end gives each diary a source, a primary work order and an application to this project. Some entries are release announcements, art diaries, hotfixes or prototypes. They inform presentation, compatibility or testing. The gameplay decisions below use the relevant design diaries and later revisions. Source coverage combines publisher pages, Steam announcements, indexed extracts and republications of developer text where a source page exposed only a title. The budget, timing, Punjab scenarios and phone interaction rules are proposals for **The Long Century**.

### Six changes to the first design

**1. Give a grain shortage three possible causes.** A poor harvest reduces physical stocks. A blocked route leaves grain at origin. A fall in earnings leaves food on sale that labourers cannot afford. These require different policies. Tap the price to see available grain, freight capacity and the poorest households’ purchasing power together. The early market diaries (#9, #37–39, #54) explain why geography matters. The famine diary (#131) adds household vulnerability. The later commercial design (#143, #146–148) shifts the player’s job toward shaping trade through investment, terms and incentives. In the Punjab pilot, a depot changes delivery capacity, a trading privilege changes landed cost, and a relief payment changes who can buy. Each must move a different line in the ledger.

**2. Separate a factory’s location, owner and source of finance.** Autonomous investment (#71–72), companies (#92), foreign ownership (#110), nationalisation (#127) and the later company design (#147–148) answer different questions. The proposed engine stores a workplace, its ownership shares, its financing transaction and its destination for dividends separately. A Multan workshop can employ local households while Lahore investors receive the surplus. A later foreign purchase changes the dividend destination. It does not move a building or rehire its workers. Show the contrast between domestic value added, local wages and domestic income on a single project sheet.

**3. Make a failed vote worth playing.** Interests, elections and parties (#6–7, #14, #45–46) establish who has influence. Staged enactment and organisers (#80–83), movements and unequal treatment (#128–130), social hierarchy (#133) and socialist organisation (#188, #195) explain how political pressure survives a cabinet. Give the player an amendment, a constituency concession or a postponement. Record what each supporter wanted. After defeat, the bill closes, the organised demand persists, and a later government can meet it through a different law. Where offices are personal, #176 and #194 suggest a further choice: a court favourite may help the ruler enact a reform while alienating its natural supporters. Give that appointment a tenure and a succession outcome.

**4. Negotiate rights clause by clause.** Early diplomacy (#19–21, #26, #75) establishes demands and exits. Subjects, investment and lobbying (#109–113, #118) describe who can bargain and who cares. Treaty articles and subsequent changes (#146, #163, #168) support a mobile proposal sheet with separate entries for tariffs, tax retention, transit, investment and duration. Allow a private memorandum under the agreement. Only structured, accepted clauses change the simulation. Test a Company offer in which Punjab grants commercial transit, keeps tax collection and declines troop passage. The next shipment, revenue report and attempted military move should each reflect the corresponding clause.

**5. Keep strategic command and revise how wars end.** Use the early war diaries (#22–26, #44) for costs, fronts and settlement choices. Later formation and naval work (#77, #93, #145, #175, #177–178) gives those decisions persistent assets and logistical limits. The AI and war-support work (#185–186, #191, #195) changes the peace rule. Support can fall or recover. Falling support carries domestic and military costs. With both sides exhausted and no attainable gain, the AI must evaluate compromise or a stalemate settlement. A single score crossing zero cannot dispose of a province. On a phone, expose the remaining demand, willingness to settle, foreseeable cost of one more quarter and the cabinet’s alternatives.

**6. Put the consequences where the player looks.** Use UX, tutorial, chart, data and map diaries (#29–30, #49–52, #61, #74, #104, #117, #121, #134) to design a mobile flow. From the Cabinet, tap “Families cannot afford grain” to open a cause sheet, then tap “Compare relief” to weigh A, B and C. Show the next month’s receipt under the same issue. Identity, movement, cost and ownership have text labels as well as visual cues. Art and music diaries inform a separate, optional presentation pass. Their visual assets and desktop screen arrangements have no dependency in the simulation.

## 1. The experience to build

Play the cabinet of a country. The interesting question each quarter is **who benefits from the last decision, and what does that make possible next?** If Punjab finances irrigation, the works compete for labour and materials today. The finished canals may raise farm output next year. Whether cultivators eat better depends on prices, rents, wages and who owns the land. If landlords capture most of the gain, their growing political weight changes the next reform debate. All of that should be visible from the original order.

Borrow Victoria 3’s connections among population, production, markets and political power. Use an original engine, original historical writing and a phone interface designed around the cabinet meeting. Paradox’s developer accounts explain the economic and political relationships, market access, escalation and information design that inform this proposal. [Society and buildings](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-57-the-journey-so-far) · [Market access and pricing](https://forumcontent.paradoxplaza.com/public/710077/Victoria%203%20-%20Vickynomics.pdf) · [Negotiating demands](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-75-diplomatic-improvements-in-1-2) · [Population and notification design](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-74-ux-improvements) · [Ownership, trade, treaties and later design diaries](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news)

**A typical five-minute session**

1. Resume to the date, last decision, funded commitments, next milestone and most urgent issue. Choose a national ambition, such as sovereignty and broad prosperity.
2. Tap an issue. “Grain costs more in Multan” opens local harvest, freight, household expenditure and a comparison with Lahore. Tap a cause to inspect the underlying figures.
3. Make a decision or let an existing order run. A/B/C choices work for political dilemmas. Construction, taxation and trade also have direct controls. Show upfront cost, recurring cost, affected groups, uncertainty and the next review date before confirmation.
4. Advance one month or one quarter. A quarter runs three real monthly turns. Pause before a binding decision, a commitment that would break a spending rule, or a crisis deadline. The player can adjust routine alerts.
5. See at most three material changes, each linked to a receipt, a province or an agreement. An annual review compares goals with actual results. At 1936, the campaign book shows territory, prosperity by class, institutions, treaties, projects and turning points. A peaceful sovereign state can earn a strong outcome.

## 2. What changes in the existing project

The reviewed release has 62 starting nation records and 184 provinces. Punjab owns Lahore & the Punjab, Multan, and the combined Kashmir & Peshawar province. Parts of Company-era India are currently assigned directly to the British tag. The economic engine starts from a world goods price and applies national tariff adjustments. Population groups, factory recipes, elections, theatre assignments, regional decisions and the Punjab challenge already run. Use the existing geography for the first release. Document combined regions as abstractions.

| Area | Starting point in vic/ | Overhaul decision |
|---|---|---|
| World and households | src/world.js, defs.js, state.js, society.js | Keep province IDs, geography and population continuity. Add governorships, markets, workplaces, household accounts and migration records. |
| Industry and money | src/econ.js, build.js | Reuse goods, recipes, construction and research where they fit. Replace the common price path with local clearing and freight constraints, and link all payments to accounts. |
| State and external affairs | src/politics.js, diplo.js, mil.js, theatre.js | Retain functioning elections, theatres and treaty effects as regression fixtures. Introduce organised interests, negotiated legislation, escalation and enforceable agreements through shared rules. |
| History and continuity | src/events.js, history.js, regional.js, greatwar.js, campaign.js | Keep earned achievements, historical branches and project records. Port decisions into explicit historical chains with conditions, ownership checks and expiry. |
| Presentation and saves | src/ui.js through ui7.js, ui.css, ui3.css, turn.js | Move simulation-side work out of screen handlers. Replace page-by-page navigation with a cabinet, atlas, economy and government plus linked detail sheets. Keep offline HTML export and an inspectable source package. |

**First milestone.** Punjab, January 1836–January 1838. Build a working canal or grain depot, finance it, employ households, deliver output, observe a price and distributional effect, and use that change in a land-revenue negotiation. Save in the middle and reproduce the result. The rest of the world continues under the existing rules until each replacement can be switched on coherently for a whole test market. A boundary account records every import, export and payment crossing the pilot market so the old and new economies cannot both book the same flow.

## 3. Simulation and gameplay rules

| System | Decisions the player makes | How consequences become playable |
|---|---|---|
| Households, jobs and land | Fund schools, recognise rights, change land tenure, permit migration or support subsistence farming. | Group households by place, occupation and relevant demographic attributes. Track workers, dependants, qualifications, earnings, taxes, essential needs and savings. Farms compete with subsistence for land and labour. Literacy opens better-paid jobs with a lag. Show living standards for poorer households alongside the national average. |
| Buildings, technology and ownership | Build, expand, privatise, subsidise or change a workshop’s production method. Choose research and fund local adoption. | Every workplace has vacancies, wage offers, input orders, expected sales, reserves and named owners. Distinguish state, private, cooperative and foreign shares. Distribute realised surplus to owners after wages and costs. Mechanisation saves labour only if the new inputs arrive. Pilot grain, timber, tools, textiles and transport, then add coal, iron, steel, engines, electricity and oil as chains become usable. |
| Markets and trade | Fund a trade centre or depot, grant a targeted trading privilege, expand freight capacity, change tariffs or subsidise a critical import. | Let trade centres place affordable orders under the cabinet’s general and treaty policies. Clear orders against available supply. Price responds to unmet demand and surplus within tuned bounds. Freight limits shipment volume and adds cost. Poorly connected states can face scarcity in a well-supplied national market. Show origin price, transport, tariff, delivered price and quantity actually received. Foreign trade competes for capacity with domestic needs. |
| Treasury, credit and investment | Set taxes and public wages, borrow, invest, change queue priority or protect a treasury reserve. | Separate treasury, household, firm and investment accounts. Display monthly balance and cash on hand. Charge infrastructure through dated milestones. Private investment follows profitability and available funds. A government can finish a low-return hospital for public benefit. Debt service can crowd out projects and default changes subsequent access to credit. |
| Politics and administration | Form a cabinet, bargain over legislation, expand local tax administration, fund institutions or concede a demand. | Landowners, commercial groups, rural communities, urban labour, officials, religious institutions and military interests draw support from actual constituencies. Use country-appropriate names and eligibility. Wealth, law and suffrage shape influence. Publish the coalition, its demands, the cost of a concession and how votes move. A law needs an implementing budget and provincial administrative reach. |
| Diplomacy, empire and war | Specify a demand, choose the terms of a guarantee, offer concessions, remain neutral, mobilise, seek mediation or settle. | Track separate rights for trade, tax collection, military access and foreign policy. A subject relationship can change one right at a time. Escalation gives each side a deadline and an exit. Keep theatre-level objectives, reserves and naval assignments. Show which peace terms are attainable. Allow war support to recover. Sustained exhaustion increases pressure to compromise. Recruitment draws workers and public funds. Supply and mobilisation stress feed back into prices, casualties, debt and support for peace. |
| History, AI and replay | Choose an ambition, a constitutional route or an approach to a crisis, and delegate recurring orders. | AI uses the same costs, eligibility and settlement rules. A weak indebted cabinet and a wealthy autonomous one should react differently to the same proposal. Record the deciding interest and the actual result. Standing orders have priority, spending limits and review dates. A breach pauses for the player. |

**How the first market clears.** Carry opening inventories and outstanding commitments into the month. Use last month’s prices to submit affordable orders. Reserve freight, match orders, deliver inputs, then produce goods, pay wages, distribute sales income and close accounts. Put new output in the next available selling period under one documented convention. Group orders at equal priority proportionally, with deterministic remainder handling. Household relief is a budgeted purchase, and every route has a counterparty. Use small fixtures before tuning a world run. In the first fixture a closed crossing reduces deliveries to Multan while grain remains available at origin. In the second, new freight capacity restores some deliveries, raises producer receipts and lowers the scarcity premium. Show both outcomes in the market screen.

**How politics becomes strategy.** A land assessment reform may raise assessed revenue while reducing landlords’ discretionary collections. An assembly can support an appeal procedure, the treasury must pay assessors and a district with too few officials collects less than its assessment. The decision screen should distinguish assessed revenue from collections. A player who funded education earlier may now have the clerks to implement a policy that a richer but understaffed neighbour cannot administer.

## 4. Portrait interface

Design against **360 × 740 and 390 × 844 CSS pixels**, then test on a physical Galaxy A56-class Android phone. Assume one thumb, intermittent sessions and large system text. Keep the primary action in the lower reach zone. Give the interface its own visual identity with an editorial atlas, ink-coloured type, paper surfaces, restrained indigo accents and a separate high-contrast dark palette. Use an original flat vector map with a single thematic layer at a time.

| Screen | First view | Touch and return behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Cabinet | Country and date, urgent decision or pinned goal, treasury trend, household pressure, next milestone. Compact bottom action reads “Advance to April” or “Review treaty”. | One tap opens the issue. A second reveals the relevant control. On a quiet month the pinned programme takes the lead. |
| Atlas | Search, current map layer, selected province, one local issue. Layers for control, market access, living standards, administration and conflict. | Tap Multan for its province sheet. Tap its project to inspect the ledger. Back returns to the same zoom, layer and province. Search and zoom buttons provide alternatives to gestures. |
| Economy | Budget, market or works view. Lead with the budget change, a binding shortage or the next construction milestone. | Tap grain → delivered-price breakdown → freight bottleneck. Compare two projects in stacked rows with identical units. Pin one metric to the Cabinet. |
| Government | Domestic and foreign agendas, upcoming vote, treaty deadlines and defence commitments. | Tap a bill to compare supporters and concessions. For a crisis, compare settlement, neutrality and escalation terms on one sheet. |
| Cabinet decision sheet | Situation, affected place, deadline and A/B/C choices. Selecting a choice reveals cost now, recurring commitment, beneficiaries and risks. | Explicit “Confirm B” commits once. Back dismisses an unconfirmed selection. A dated receipt links to the chronicle. Pending decisions survive app suspension. |
| Records and resume | Last choice, active orders, committed spending, completed assets and nearest deadline. Open from the Cabinet header. | Find an old hospital by name, tap to see its original cost and current service. Annual reports link to the same ledger. |

**Phone acceptance.** Four labelled bottom destinations. Allow 48 × 48 CSS pixel touch areas for primary controls. Put the month control immediately above the navigation bar. At largest text, allow a vertically scrolling sheet with a reachable close and confirm button, respect system insets and restore focus after Back. Charts have tap-to-read values and text equivalents. Use shape or hatching for occupation and unrest. Routine news groups into a digest. Mandatory choices interrupt advance exactly once. Test portrait with the keyboard open, offline relaunch, TalkBack reading order and return from background. Record taps and wrong turns on three tasks: find why grain rose, queue a funded project, and explain the last treaty.

## 5. Historical campaign

**Punjab gets the first authored campaign.** It exposes the most consequential limitation of the starting map: the Lahore government, Company authorities, neighbouring states and Britain need distinct interests and powers. Model the Company as a governing actor with specified rights and a relationship to London. Assign provinces and princely relationships from a dated research sheet. Represent the 1858 transfer only where Company authority still exists. The Treaty of Lahore and the later transfer of Company administrative powers supply historical reference points. |---|---|---|
| Punjab and Company India, 1836–1851 | Court succession, army pay, provincial revenue, contested authority, treaty restrictions and the 1849 annexation. | A funded succession settlement and solvent cabinet can retain sovereignty at a real political price. A dependent cabinet may renegotiate tax or diplomatic rights. A victorious independent Punjab never receives an annexed-state narrative. January 1851 scores sovereignty and one chosen domestic ambition. Continue the same save. |
| Japan and Persia, mid-to-late century | External demands, reform coalitions and the costs of administering new institutions. | Japan may bargain over ports, delay an opening or fund adoption after political reform. Persia’s budget dispute depends on who controls appropriations. A prior refusal and an existing assembly alter the next choice. [Japan opening reference](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1830-1860/opening-to-japan) |
| Industrial Europe and the Americas | Unification, franchise politics, emancipation, rail investment, export dependence and labour organisation. | Test Prussia against a fragmented German region, US emancipation against a divergent legislature, and Chile under concentrated versus diversified exports. A change of flag preserves the population, debt and ownership that actually transfer. |
| Imperial rivalry, East Asia and Africa | Treaty interpretation, recognition, protectorates, resistance and unequal market access. | Ethiopia’s diplomatic dispute requires a relevant signatory and contested terms. Korea’s response depends on actual sovereignty. Offer domestic options to the affected population and a separate decision to the authority able to act. |
| Mass politics and global shocks, 1900–1936 | Alliance crises, wartime finance, postwar settlement, epidemics, independence movements and credit contraction. | A mediated crisis can end peacefully. War burdens fall on participants, and neutral suppliers face their own trade-offs. Depression exposure follows export concentration, creditors and banks. An independent Punjab has its own popular politics alongside protests in British-administered India. [Financial transmission reference](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/banking-panics-1931-33) |

Use two play settings. **Historical pressures** favours historically documented tensions when their prerequisites exist. **Open history** gives more weight to current interests. Both honour prior choices and actual institutions. Every authored chain needs a dated source, the governing actor, triggers, mutually exclusive branches, AI priorities, expiry, domestic consequences and two fixtures showing historical and divergent conditions. A simulated newspaper entry clearly distinguishes the documented historical background from what happened in this campaign. For initial populations, production and revenues, record year, unit, source and confidence. Mark uncertain estimates and tune them separately from documented dates and treaty terms.

## 6. Engine, storage and performance

- **One authoritative monthly transition.** Track ownership, administration and market membership independently from a province’s stable ID. Route funded orders through commands with actor, target, date, unique ID and validation. Use an ordered simulation step for settlement, economy, households, politics, diplomacy, history and reports. Display code reads snapshots. Previewing a choice cannot spend money, consume random numbers or award score. Profile before moving expensive steps to a Web Worker. A worker and main-thread fallback must produce identical results.
- **Stable world identity.** Keep IDs for provinces, governing authorities, countries, markets, households, firms, projects and treaties. Distinguish legal sovereignty, administration, occupation, ownership and market membership. Place-changing events transfer precisely the rights and assets named in the agreement. Track stock measures and monthly flows separately.
- **Accountable saves.** Add schema, ruleset and historical-data versions, seed and random-generator state, unresolved decisions, orders, project liabilities and a compact transaction journal. Import into a validated temporary state, simulate a read-only summary, then commit atomically. Preserve a pre-upgrade export. Where older saves lack ownership, show an explicit migration assumption. Keep the patched ruleset playable for campaigns whose historical state cannot be reconstructed faithfully.
- **Offline persistence.** Use IndexedDB for transactional snapshots and a bounded journal, with a manual file export/import flow that works for a locally opened HTML build. Verify capability at startup. Keep two recoverable checkpoints, cap detailed history with annual aggregates, and retain permanent receipts for assets and treaties. Test storage refusal, interruption during a write and two tabs competing to save one campaign.
- **Cost of a turn.** Preserve the project’s 130 ms average engine-month target on a documented reference machine. Measure all eight original seeds sequentially through 1,200 turns. Separately measure button-to-interactive time including storage on the target phone. Proposed device targets are p95 under 500 ms for a single turn and visible progress for longer advances. Record version, device, sample size, p50/p95, save size and failed writes. Benchmark early, middle and late saves. The previous 50.2 ms short run covered one seed and engine time only.
- **Determinism and balance.** Compare uninterrupted play, restart and save/reload at a pending dilemma, market shock and active war. Track defaults, price volatility, living standards by class, state survival, wars and peaceful settlements by seed. An extreme result calls for an explanation and replay fixture. It can be a legitimate outcome of player choices.
