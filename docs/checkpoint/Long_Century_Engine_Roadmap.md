# The Long Century · Engine assessment and development roadmap

**Design decision.** Keep the offline, monthly, 1836–1936 web game. Make production, household welfare and political power respond to the same transactions in every country. The first playable release should let Punjab respond to a grain shortage through trade, transport, taxation or relief, then show who ate better, who paid and who changed political allegiance. Run the identical scenario under a different flag.

## 1 · What the checkpoint actually does

Baseline inspected: Long_Century_Performance_Checkpoint.zip, including its source, engine manifest, tests and century-run logs. The 22 November map and art document is a design brief. Its findings inform the interaction budget. This assessment does not assume its proposed changes have shipped.

| Finding | Evidence and consequence | Engineering decision |
|---|---|---|
| The simulation already runs independently of rendering | engine-files.json lists 17 engine files. load_test_engine.js compiles those files together. src/state.js provides seeded randomness. src/turn.js advances economy, society, research, politics, events, diplomacy and the armed forces. | Keep the browser and headless paths on the same code. Add named phase inputs and outputs incrementally. Separate the decision to default an unanswered card from the presence of a UI global. |
| Choosing a flag changes the rules of the world | src/econ.js selects Punjab and the current player for five provincial household goods. src/society.js uses hasProvincialEconomy for employment and household handling. | There are already several working market layers | econMonth clears national supply and demand, sphere claims, bilateral orders and world residuals. settleLocalGood redistributes a household pool derived from national fill. | Construction has ledgers and queue controls, but control changes matter | transferProv in src/politics.js cancels active builds, marks matching ledger records and clears the province queue of active works. ## 2 · Integrate the monthly engine

**One owner per fact.** Geography owns adjacency and control. Population owns people and qualifications. Firms own jobs, production methods and operating reserves. Markets own deliveries and price formation. Finance owns money transfers and arrears. Construction owns work remaining and commissioning. Politics owns institutions and binding laws. Diplomacy owns treaty clauses. War owns theatre commitments. Reports read these records. A law changes a tax rule once and the treasury collects the resulting receipts. The dashboard sums those receipts.

| Proposed monthly phase | Reads | Writes and timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Accept decisions | Last closed month, issued commands, treaty terms, expiry dates | Validate authority, available fiscal headroom and prerequisites. Reserve commitments atomically. Settle ties in a published deterministic order. |
| 2. Form plans | Opening inventories, prices, expected wages, last month's shortages | Employers offer jobs, workers seek vacancies, owners submit investment plans, projects and theatres request deliveries. Budget each claimant. |
| 3. Produce and clear | Opening input inventories, staffed capacity, household budgets, transport graph | Produce from inputs on hand, match orders and allocate freight. Receipts identify buyer, seller, origin, destination, quantity, unit price, freight, duty and unmet quantity. Imported industrial inputs can feed next month's production. |
| 4. Settle accounts | Deliveries, contracts, available income, statutory rates | Post wages, purchases, dividends, taxes, debt service and transfers once. Record credit or arrears where needed. Settle household consumption. |
| 5. Advance slow state | Delivered inputs, real incomes, work crews, research and service funding | Progress construction, migration, qualifications, institutions, politics and theatre readiness. Commission once at month close. New capacity contributes from the following month. |
| 6. Close and explain | All receipts, demographic flows, project transitions, political changes | Check balances, compute histories and compact cause records, update AI beliefs, schedule next choices, advance clock and save the closed state. |

During migration, preserve the original order for domains still using the legacy tick. Introduce a new phase behind a test switch, compare its outputs, then cut over all consumers of a good together. An order arriving after close is dated for the next open month. Previewing and cancelling a preview must consume no campaign RNG and write no state.

**Minimum durable records.** Give projects, assets, employers, treaties and accounts stable IDs. A project carries sponsor, site, funding source, ordered quantity, paid-to-date, delivered materials, remaining work, status, disposition and commissioned asset ID. A standing order carries budget ceiling, priority, expiry and last execution. Use a bounded monthly explanation buffer and retain annual totals plus permanent project/treaty transitions. Rebuild indexes on load. Extend the existing save migrator, recording unknown legacy spending as unknown. Keep the existing annual payment summaries and do not fabricate historical receipts.

**Accounting checks.** Opening stock + production + imports = intermediate use + household use + project use + exports + recorded losses + closing stock. A shipment appears once at each endpoint. Every payment credits a named counterparty or an explicit external account. National GDP sums domestic value added, including consistently valued nonmarket output, and excludes intermediate goods already counted. Each original project ID has one live state or one terminal disposition. The sum of ownership shares equals one. Household counts change only through documented demographic flows. A blocked route must change deliveries throughout the game.

## 3 · System work orders

The diary numbers identify design readings. The mechanics and acceptance fixtures in this table are proposals for The Long Century. Land one small playable change per row before expanding its content catalogue.

| System and reading | Decision and implementation | Integration and acceptance fixture |
|---|---|---|
| Geography, states and freight · #37, #143 | Keep provinces for player interaction. Aggregate adjacent provinces into market regions for clearing. Cache a sparse network of rail, river and sea links with capacity, cost and controller. Recompute affected links on construction or control changes. | Two grain regions with the same harvest have different landed costs across a narrow link. Upgrading that link increases deliveries up to the next bottleneck. Map and Works identify the same link. |
| Households, employment and qualifications · #57, #74, #105 | Extend existing population groups with dependants and a compact skill profile. Budget food first and allow affordable substitutes. Hire qualified workers into funded vacancies, then train or promote on a slower cadence. Merge small equivalent cohorts under a documented tolerance. | Trace a mill wage into food purchases and tax. A funded mill can stay understaffed. A migrant leaving one province appears in the receiving province exactly once. |
| Agriculture, land, harvests and famine · #81, #131 | Give farms tenants or owner-operators, rent, crop mix, harvest exposure and storage. Seasons change output. Sustained unmet food needs generate famine pressure. Irrigation, imports and relief affect different points in that chain. | In paired harvest shocks, imported grain can relieve a coastal state while an isolated interior district remains short. A silo can distribute only the amount it previously stored. |
| Industry and production methods · #57, #59, #78 | Extend recipes with input quantities, occupations, energy and output. Owners forecast a method switch after accounting for installation time, training and input prices. Offer a national default with provincial exceptions. | A technically advanced textile mill earns less when fuel or engineers are scarce. The forecast names the binding constraint and explains any difference from realised profit. |
| Markets, prices and trade · #37, #38, #143 | Clear local output, domestic exchange and residual international orders in succession. Support flexible household substitution, landed prices and actual tariff bases. Allow standing export orders with a domestic reserve threshold. | A higher foreign bid can lose after freight and duty. A customs exemption removes only the applicable duty. Reconcile each good through all tiers even when supply is zero. |
| Construction and public works · construction diary, #71, #74 | Reuse the central Works planner. Give public and private queues access to the same crews and materials. Show stock, spending limit, expected completion range and the project displaced by moving one to the front. | Start a railway, add a mill and encounter a timber shortage. Reload, reprioritise and transfer the province. Every pound spent and every commissioned asset remains attributable. Rapid double-tapping commits once. |
| Ownership, private investment and companies · #71, #110, #127, #147 | Build on existing investment pools and foreign dividends. Begin with public, domestic private and foreign ownership shares. Companies then acquire sites, retain profits and bid for capacity under specific charter rights. | A foreign-financed mill raises host GDP while part of its profit goes abroad. Nationalisation transfers shares at the agreed price or creates an explicit dispute. Private project demand can raise the state's construction bill. |
| Treasury, credit and financial crises · #57, #71 | Extend moneyMonth, credit limits and defaults with a maturity schedule, creditor exposure and budget commitments. Show free treasury after reservations. Institutions, projects and military budgets compete for recurring funds. | Interest-rate changes reach refinancing as debts mature. A default records creditor losses. A rich treasury can still face a shortage of skilled workers. |
| Technology, education and diffusion · #57, #59 | Keep research progress and unlocks. Add adoption at the employer or institution: affordability, literacy, qualified workers and connections determine when a discovery changes outcomes. | Two countries discover the same method. Only the one with inputs and trained staff initially gains output. A college improves later adoption and its effect can be traced to local graduates. |
| Laws, parties, elections and interest groups · #57, #81, #128 | Derive support from occupations, ownership, franchise, organisation and geography. Keep durable group identities and issue-specific movements. Introduce proposal, coalition bargaining, passage and implementation. | A land-tax bill can command a numerical majority and still fail under a restricted franchise. Expanding the franchise changes influence using the same population ledger. |
| Administration, institutions and welfare · #57, #74 | Set national service targets for education, health and tax collection. Provincial buildings, staff and funding determine actual coverage. Publish the marginal benefit and recurring cost of expanding service. | Raising the education law with no teachers creates a coverage gap. Funding schools moves attendance before literacy. An automated standing order fills funded vacancies within its ceiling. |
| Identity, migration, radicalism and internal conflict · #105, #128–131, #133 | Model legal eligibility, local acceptance, wage prospects, moving costs and destinations. Track grievances by cause and cohort. Give movements demands, organisers, concessions and the ability to lose support. Regional hierarchies belong in scenario data. | Equal wages with unequal legal access yield different migration. Addressing food prices reduces the subsistence grievance while a disenfranchisement movement can persist. |
| Diplomacy, subjects, blocs and empire · #58, #109, #111–113, #118, #146 | Build clauses for transit, tariffs, investment rights, transfers and guarantees. Give each side an outside option, implementation cost and review date. Subjects weigh local legitimacy, fiscal transfers, autonomy and credible alternatives. Colonial expansion consumes capacity and creates local political consequences. | A transit-for-access bargain can benefit both parties. When the route closes, trade and treaty value both fall. A small subject can rationally reject a profitable clause that gives away too much autonomy. |
| Nationalism, recognition and unification · #57, #113–115 | Split eligibility, leadership competition, accession votes, external response and integration into persistent stages. Model mutual incentives for peaceful federation and the costs of imposed integration. | Prussia cannot absorb a resisting member solely through its sphere status. A credible, politically supported early union remains possible. A rival claimant can block, bargain or lose. |
| Army, navy, theatre supply and peace · #145, #175 | Preserve theatre-level orders. Formations draw recruits from households, readiness from delivered stores and sustainment from transports. Fleets compete for fuel, dockyards and trained crews. Peace compares attainable settlement packages with continuing costs. | Mobilisation raises public spending and reduces civilian labour. A fleet under refit occupies a dockyard slot. A disrupted route changes readiness and the value of reaching terms. Surface these as strategic decisions. |
| Events, journals and historical programmes · #57, #114–115, #136 | Give campaigns persistent stage, eligible actors, material prerequisites, expiry and counterfactual branches. Generate card promises from the same effect descriptions used to settle them. Historical scripts issue normal authorised commands. | Change ownership or dissolve a required state before the trigger. The entry branches or expires cleanly. A funded programme persists through a cabinet change and can close with a documented partial outcome. |
| Cabinet, map, construction controls and player advice · #59, #74, #117 | Supply all views from committed results and forecast queries. On each major decision show feasible choices, beneficiaries, cost, likely reply, time to effect and recommendation. One map-to-Works entry should retain province and filter across repeat builds. | At 360 px and 125% text, identify a shortage, compare import/rail/relief and queue the chosen action without revisiting tabs. Report magnitude, cause and next decision in the monthly summary. |

## 4 · Give actors strategic reasoning

**Separate technical decisions from strategic interaction.** A household choosing bread or a substitute needs a cheap budgeting rule. A firm deciding whether to build needs a forecast that includes other projects already underway. A treaty or coalition requires reasoning about someone else's response. Reserve opponent modelling for these interactions.

| Actor | Objective and constraints | Reasoning cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Household cohort | Satisfy needs, retain a buffer, improve earnings subject to skill, legal access and moving cost | Budget monthly. Reconsider migration when the wage or access gap persists. |
| Employer and owner | Risk-adjusted discounted profit after finance, labour, input and freight costs | Refresh operating decisions monthly. Rank investment alternatives quarterly or after a material shock. |
| Cabinet | Solvency, welfare, legitimacy, commitments and its declared programme | Maintain a short list of policy bundles. Re-evaluate after elections, shortages, defaults or treaty changes. |
| Political group | Member income, rights, representation and credibility with supporters | Compare support, amendment, abstention and opposition at each bill stage. Check coalition partners' outside options. |
| Foreign government | Security, autonomy, trade income, domestic approval and reliability | Evaluate accept, counter, defer or refuse. Update beliefs from observed actions. Give each side its own information. |

**Implementation sequence.** Start with existing scoring functions to find the best few legal alternatives plus wait. Cache an actor's observed facts and their ages. Evaluate the marginal effects of each candidate against waiting, including already committed projects. For negotiations, estimate the counterpart's response to each candidate. Evaluate each response branch separately. Charge risk and broken commitments once. Retain the selected option, runner-up, decisive term, uncertainty and the condition that would reverse the recommendation. Use a deterministic action budget per actor. Stable ordering and isolated forecast randomness keep opening the adviser from changing history.

**Worked bargaining fixture.** Illustrative utilities for a transit treaty are expressed in each cabinet's own units, relative to waiting. They are test inputs, not measured game values.

| Punjab offers | Partner accepts | Partner counters | Partner refuses |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Request free transit | Punjab +8, partner −3 | Punjab +3, partner +2 | 0, 0 |
| B. Offer a modest fee and a two-year minimum payment | Punjab +5, partner +4 | Punjab +2, partner +3 | Punjab −1, partner 0 |

With complete information and these continuation values, the partner counters A and accepts B. Punjab prefers B (+5 over +3). For a bounded-information fixture, assign illustrative response probabilities of 10/55/35% for A and 70/20/10% for B. Punjab's expected gains are 2.45 and 3.8. The adviser should recommend B and name the assumption that matters. Close an alternative route, lower trust, or make a domestic veto binding and recalculate both parties' outside options. In the live game, show probability ranges only after forecast calibration. A deterministic, known legal veto should display as a veto.

**Further games worth implementing.** In private investment, compare building first, waiting or entering a different industry when a rival mill is funded. In legislation, exchange concessions only if every pivotal group prefers the feasible deal to defeat. In repeated treaties, track promise fulfilment, capacity to honour it and the value of future cooperation separately. In crises, model a negotiated exit, a counteroffer and persistence costs for both sides. Log which prediction changed the decision. Compare against the current AI with identical opening states and held-out seeds.

**Quality measures.** Compare predicted vs realised cash flow over 3 and 12 months. For counterparty decisions, score probability forecasts against actual choices using a Brier score and reliability bins with sample counts. Compare welfare, insolvencies, variety of viable strategies, treaty durability and decision time against the heuristic baseline. Include a cheap always-refuse and an always-accept baseline. Audit whether a policy is repeatedly selected because of a missing cost. Report poor forecasts by situation, especially small states and unusual scenarios.

## 5 · Playable milestones and release gates

| Release | Dependency and scope | Demonstration required for sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| A · Equal rules and durable works | Instrument monthTick. Generalise actor commands. Repair participant selection, test employment parity and ownership transfer, then widen the five-good pilot within budget. | Identical state, commands and RNG give the same material result with a different spectator. Queue a new project while another is partially paid, reload, change ownership and reconcile both. |
| B · Food, wages and freight | Requires A. Deliver region clearing, freight, household receipts, harvests and one import option. | Replay a Punjab harvest shock with emergency imports, a rail commitment, targeted relief and waiting. Compare the first three months and the next two years, including fiscal and distributional effects. |
| C · Industrialisation and ownership | Requires B prices, deliveries and jobs. Deliver one textile chain, education-to-skills pipeline, competing investments and company ownership. | An investor and cabinet can rationally prefer different sites. A queued rival mill can reverse a previously attractive project. National accounts reconcile. |
| D · Representative politics | Requires B household budgets and C ownership. Add influence, a land-tax bill, a coalition and local service coverage. | Show who supports the tax and why, how a concession changes the pivotal vote, and how the enacted policy changes net incomes and later politics. |
| E · External bargaining | Requires B freight and D domestic mandates. Ship transit clauses, repeated promises, subject consent and negotiation forecasts. | Exporters and domestic consumers disagree on an agreement. Actual routes and payments honour the signed clauses. A poor offer elicits a plausible counteroffer. |
| F · State formation and theatre integration | Requires logistics, domestic legitimacy and treaties. Add unification stages, formation sustainment, naval capacity and revised peace valuation. | Run a disputed unification, a negotiated settlement and continuation under supply stress. Track territory, depleted workforces, asset ownership and obligations through peace. |
| G · Full campaign and content expansion | Expand goods, actors and journals only after their underlying rows pass. Add comparable cases for an industrial power, a landlocked minor, a subject and a late industrialiser. | Keep old saves loadable. Benchmark through 1936, test Android portrait, and publish historical frequency ranges and representative counterfactuals. |

**First engineering tickets, in merge order**

1. Add an observer-neutral fixture and a prior-save fixture with an active part-paid project. Capture per-phase time, state digest and balances at fixed dates. Use the normal campaign entry and headless path with the same card policy.
2. Generalise command authority. Keep the existing browser wrapper, give AI and events stable issuer IDs, and test command retries against save/reload. Move one rail command end to end before extending the pattern.
3. Unify project transitions for every payer and project kind. Record transfer cancellation or succession, ensure completion creates exactly one asset and ensure new orders cannot erase previously committed work.
4. Replace the hard-coded country selection in both economy and society. Start with five goods globally, reuse sparse active rows and benchmark. Toggling the selected seat must affect presentation alone when actor actions are fixed.
5. Introduce a grain delivery receipt and input/output reconciliation. Use one receipt path for relief, rail allocation and imports. Build the comparison scenario before expanding the commodity list.
6. Render the three alternatives and waiting in the existing Works/cabinet flow. Save the forecast alongside the decision, then compare it with realised outcomes. Capture headless and wrapped-browser acceptance results together.

**Release gates.** Preserve exact digests for refactors and preview-only changes. For intentional economic changes, record a new model version, balance deltas and reviewed invariants. A legacy save must load with its treasury, completed assets, live queues, treaties and credible defaults. Compare archived seeds 3, 7, 11 and 21 and add held-out seeds. Record whether unification arose through consent, conflict or transfer. Review distributions by country type rather than tuning a single seed to an exact year. Keep the existing 130 ms desktop Chromium month, 1 MB endgame save and 4 MB four-slot budgets. Log p50, p95 and worst month separately, plus cold load and autosave latency. Verify the wrapped game and actual Android portrait at 360/390 px and 100/125% text. If clearing exceeds budget, shrink the active graph, cache shared costs or stagger discretionary AI reviews. Keep identical rules for each actor.
